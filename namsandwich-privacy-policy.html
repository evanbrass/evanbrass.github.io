Privacy Policy — NAM Sandwich

Effective date: June 27, 2026  ·  Last updated: August 18, 2026

NAM Sandwich (“the app”) is a guitar amp-modeling app for iOS and Android developed by Evan Brass (“we”, “us”). This policy covers both versions; where they differ, the platform is named. We designed the app to be private by default: your playing and your settings stay on your device. This policy explains exactly what the app accesses, why, and what leaves your device (very little).

If you have any questions, contact us at evanbrass@gmail.com.

Short version

  • Your guitar audio is processed live on your device and is never recorded, stored, or transmitted.
  • The app has no analytics, no tracking, no advertising, and no developer-run servers.
  • The only data that leaves your device is when you choose to sign in to and download tones from Tone3000, when Apple or Google processes an optional purchase, and — on iOS only, if you leave it on — when your own library syncs to your iCloud account.
  • Your captures, sandwiches (presets), and settings are stored on your device (and, on iOS, in your private iCloud database if iCloud is enabled on the device).

What the app accesses

Microphone (guitar input)

The app needs microphone access to receive your instrument signal (typically through an audio interface) so it can run it through the amp models in real time. This audio is processed live, in memory, on your device. It is not recorded to a file, not saved, and not sent anywhere. Turning off microphone permission simply stops audio from flowing through the app.

Tone3000 (optional, only when you use it)

The app can browse and download .nam and impulse-response files from Tone3000 (tone3000.com). This happens only when you tap to sign in or download:

  • Sign-in uses Tone3000’s standard OAuth flow in a secure system web view. We do not see or store your Tone3000 password. The app stores only the access token needed to download files you request.
  • When you download a tone, the app requests that file from Tone3000 over an encrypted connection and saves it locally to your library.
  • Tone3000 is operated by a third party and has its own privacy policy and content terms, which govern your account and the files you download. Please review Tone3000’s policies for how they handle your account data.

We do not send your personal information to Tone3000 beyond what its sign-in flow requires, and we do not receive your Tone3000 account details.

Purchases (optional, handled by Apple or Google)

If you buy the annual plan or the one-time unlock, the transaction is handled entirely by Apple (App Store, using your Apple Account) or Google (Google Play, using your Google account), depending on where you installed the app. We do not receive or store your name, payment card, or billing details, and we run no purchase-verification server of our own. The store’s own privacy policy governs the transaction. We only read the entitlement status the store reports on your device, so the app can unlock paid functionality.

Android: notification, foreground service, and USB

On Android the app declares a few permissions that exist purely to keep audio working:

  • Microphone, as above.
  • A foreground service (and the notification that Android requires with it) when “Keep playing in background” is on. Android silences microphone input for apps that aren’t visible, so without it the amp goes quiet the moment you switch away. The notification says only that the amp is running. Turning the setting off stops the service and the notification.
  • Notifications, requested only so that notification can be displayed. Declining it changes nothing else about the app.
  • USB, so the app can be offered when you plug in an audio interface, and can open a low-latency stream on it. The app reads no data from USB devices other than audio.

On-device storage

Your downloaded captures and IR files, your saved sandwiches (presets), performance slots, and app settings are stored locally on your device, using the platform’s own on-device storage (SwiftData on iOS, Room and shared preferences on Android). This data is not uploaded to us or to any third party.

Note on iCloud (iOS only): the app syncs your library — captures, sandwiches, Perform slots and saved loops — to your own private iCloud database, under your Apple Account, so it reaches your other devices and survives a reinstall. It is also what the app’s Back Up Now / Restore From iCloud commands read and write. That data lives in your iCloud account, governed by Apple’s privacy policy; we have no access to it, no server of our own is involved, and you can turn it off in iOS Settings by disabling iCloud for NAM Sandwich. The Android version has no cloud sync at all — its library is device-local.

What we do NOT do

  • We do not collect analytics or usage statistics.
  • We do not use third-party tracking, advertising, or marketing SDKs.
  • We do not operate our own servers that receive your data.
  • We do not sell, rent, or share your personal information.
  • We do not record or retain your audio.

Data retention and deletion

Because your captures, presets, and settings live on your device, you control them directly. You can delete individual captures and sandwiches in the app, and deleting the app removes all of its locally stored data. On iOS, anything already synced to your iCloud account is removed by deleting it in the app or by removing NAM Sandwich’s data from iCloud in iOS Settings. Files you downloaded from Tone3000 are stored locally and are removed when you delete them or delete the app.

Children’s privacy

The app is not directed to children under 13 and does not knowingly collect personal information from children. The app collects no personal information from any user as described above.

Security

Network requests the app makes (to Tone3000, Apple, Google and — on iOS — iCloud) use encrypted (HTTPS/TLS) connections. Locally stored data is protected by your device’s standard security and file protection.

Changes to this policy

If we change how the app handles data, we will update this policy and revise the “Last updated” date above.
